Design notes & motion

What changes

  • Motion lab (v2.2). The rail's neighbours sit back as you swipe, the hero title sinks and fades as it leaves, the compact nav draws a reading-progress line, and primary controls carry haptic beats — 8 ms light, 12 ms open, a double beat on hold. Reduced flattens the depth and keeps the progress.
  • Material, not blur. Glass is now a recipe: high-saturation backdrop, a 1px specular rim and a soft ambient shadow. The nav, the floating ticket bar and the dock use the same three tokens, so chrome reads as one system.
  • Duo. The stage turns the single iPhone into an iPhone Duo: one body, two pages, hinged in the middle. The app runs on the first page; the second shows the event in focus — same photo, title, date, host — at a glance. It fits the room the stage gives it, so a 770 pt body still lands whole on a 390 pt phone. Fold swings the second page shut about the hinge and the app carries on the cover screen — the outer display is the same page, not a second design. The stage's Duo state is the class withDuo; it used to be duo, the same name as the board, so the board's display:none rule hid the whole page.
  • Editorial type. Serif for the event title, the calendar page, the price and the section numerals; sans for controls. The hierarchy is size and rhythm now, not extra rules.
  • Four frames, then a way out (v2.3). The rail shows at most four events; when a tab has more, the fifth card is a paper card holding three of the ones that did not fit, fanned like a thrown deck the first time it comes into view, and a tap opens Browse by type with its type tabs. Five frames is the ceiling NN/g puts on a carousel ("it's unlikely users will engage with more"), and the fifth is one swipe away — inside their "reach the last item in 3–4 steps" rule.
  • The Shortlist pages instead of scrolling. The list under the rail is four large rows a page — one phone screenful, which is what Baymard measures as "2–4 cards visible per viewport" and why four, not six, suits rows with a photo. It swipes on its own track (the page around it never moves), and the arrows and dots drive the same track. A tab with a single page — Tonight — shows no page navigation at all.
  • Page one is the best four. NN/g's eyetracking puts 57 % of viewing time above the fold and 74 % in the first two screenfuls, so the order inside the Shortlist is the order of the tab, not alphabetical.
  • The tabs tell the truth. Tonight is Thursday's three events; the weekend is what is actually on Saturday and Sunday. Counts in the Shortlist sub-line are read from the tab's own list, not typed in.
  • Search, balanced (v2.3). Home keeps its v2.2 header — wordmark, map, avatar — and the field still carries the area inside it. What changed is the fit: the label can shrink now and sits centred between the magnifier and the area chip, so the pill stays even end to end instead of running off the right edge.
  • Home keeps you oriented. The header fades a night wash in as the hero slides under it, so the wordmark, area, search and avatar never disappear.
  • Depth on the rail. The card nearest the centre sits at full size and its neighbours settle back 5 %, measured in client coordinates so the snapped card is always the flat one. Dots under the rail mark the position; Reduced removes the offsets and keeps the dots as information.
  • One row, one fact each. Thumbnail, kicker and time, title, place and price on one line — and the row still opens with its photo flying into the event hero. That row is the unit the Shortlist pages and the type screens list.
  • Browse by type opens a real screen. Supper club, Music, Fashion and Gallery each push in over Home (Home steps back and dims), with the type's edit copy, a count, a switcher for the other types and its own list. Back slides it away. See more opens the same screen on All, with the four type tabs at the top.
  • One origin per screen. Type rows open the same event detail, and Back returns to the list you came from — never to a screen you didn't leave.
  • The event opens from the card itself. The tapped photo flies into the hero; back reverses it.
  • One quiet nav. Over the hero, back and the pills float on glass. Past 140 pt they hand off to a compact bar with the title and the five section tabs.
  • The stub settles, it doesn't pop. It rises 22 pt after the hero lands, which reads as the ticket being printed.
  • Reserving has a beginning and an end. The ring draws while the hold is placed, then a check and the held state — never a dead tap with no feedback.
  • The ticket bar adds up. Chips set the quantity, and both the total and the CTA multiply the per-ticket price — 2 vé reads 1.700.000₫, not 850.000₫ twice over. Chips past the seats left are disabled.
  • One source per fact. The hero now carries only the photo, the type and the age. Time, place, price and seats live once, in the ticket stub; the bar carries only the running total. The age row no longer contradicts an "All ages" pill.
  • Less furniture. Good to know is a label/value grid, What's included is a numbered editorial list, and the host is one plain row — no cards and no row rules, just one hairline closing each section.
  • Save the date, in either calendar. A slim row inside the ticket adds the event to the banbe schedule (the bell turns into a spring check) or hands it to Apple Calendar — two actions in 50 pt instead of a card of their own.

Why it's easier to use

  • Every target is at least 44 pt; the nav hands off at a scroll position you can feel, not a threshold you have to hunt.
  • Motion only ever explains a change of place — nothing moves that you didn't touch, and nothing delays reading.
  • Reduced motion is a first-class state, not a fallback: Reduced above flattens every duration and removes the scroll depth, and prefers-reduced-motion does the same automatically. Reading progress stays, because it is information.
  • Text contrast moves with the surface: white over photo, ink the moment the bar turns solid.
