Two screens, one quiet system

A Liquid Glass-era pass on the two screens people spend the most time in: warmer paper, editorial type, chrome that reads as material rather than blur, and iPhone Duo and iPad stages: the same app on every screen, as list + detail when there is room for both.

Design notes & motion

What changes

  • Motion lab (v2.2). The rail's neighbours sit back as you swipe, the hero title sinks and fades as it leaves, the compact nav draws a reading-progress line, and primary controls carry haptic beats — 8 ms light, 12 ms open, a double beat on hold. Reduced flattens the depth and keeps the progress.
  • Material, not blur. Glass is now a recipe: high-saturation backdrop, a 1px specular rim and a soft ambient shadow. The nav, the floating ticket bar and the dock use the same three tokens, so chrome reads as one system.
  • iPhone Duo and iPad (v2.3.2). Duo is Apple's book-style foldable: one continuous screen. Cover screen (5.4", 466 × 678 pt) runs the app as on a phone. Inner display (7.6", 890 × 626 pt) and iPad landscape (iPad Air 11", 1180 × 820 pt) use the iPad split view: lists on the left (Home, See all, Browse by type), the event on the right. The right side is never empty — it shows the selected event, a tap on another one swaps it in place and marks it in the list, and there is no Back on that side because nothing was pushed. The left column uses a smaller rail (two events and a third peeking) so it reads as a list. iPad portrait (820 × 1180 pt) is one column like the phone, with the reading column held to 640 pt. Folding, unfolding and rotating keep what you were looking at; switching device starts clean on Home.
  • A bar that makes room (v2.3.2). The event opens with a compact one-row reserve bar — quantity and a plain Reserve button (the total is shown once, beside it, never repeated in the button) — so the whole ticket stub is readable on arrival. Scroll past 40 pt and it grows back into the full card (chips, total, the host's 1-hour line); back at the top it compacts again.
  • The header, scrolled (v2.3.2). Past 60 pt the wash turns solid, the search field folds into a search button beside the map, and the header's bottom edge is a 26 pt feather — tabs no longer show through behind the field and cards no longer lose their top corners to a hard edge.
  • Editorial type. Serif for the event title, the calendar page, the price and the section numerals; sans for controls. The hierarchy is size and rhythm now, not extra rules.
  • Four frames, then See all (v2.3.1). The rail shows at most four events; when a tab has more, the fifth card is See all. Swipe to it and it plays the iOS guest Welcome entrance: the side photos slide in from their own edge and swing into a fan, the middle one rises, then the tab's chip (For You, Weekend) arrives from below and glows gold once. It replays each time the card comes back into view. A tap lists that tab's events — "See all 8" in For You shows those 8, with type chips counted inside the tab — not the whole catalogue.
  • Browse by type is back (v2.3.1). The v2 strip closes Home: one chip per type with its real count, and See all for the whole catalogue (18).
  • Filters work. When, price, type and seats narrow the Shortlist (the rail stays the tab's picks); the sheet counts matches live, the Filters chip shows how many are on, and an empty result offers Clear filters.
  • Facts follow the event. The countdown is read from the date (Tonight / Tomorrow / In 3 days), the drink row from the event's own menu, an Age row appears only for 18+, Message names the real host, and saving an event is one state shared by the rail and the detail.
  • The Shortlist pages instead of scrolling. The list under the rail is four large rows a page — one phone screenful, which is what Baymard measures as "2–4 cards visible per viewport" and why four, not six, suits rows with a photo. It swipes on its own track (the page around it never moves), and the arrows and page numbers drive the same track. The numbers share one ink that stretches across to the new page and settles, rows slide in from the side you are travelling to, and "5–8 of 18" says where you are. Every Browse by type and See all list pages the same way. A tab with a single page — Tonight — shows no page navigation at all.
  • Page one is the best four. NN/g's eyetracking puts 57 % of viewing time above the fold and 74 % in the first two screenfuls, so the order inside the Shortlist is the order of the tab, not alphabetical.
  • The tabs tell the truth. Tonight is Thursday's three events; the weekend is what is actually on Saturday and Sunday. Counts in the Shortlist sub-line are read from the tab's own list, not typed in.
  • Search, balanced (v2.3). Home keeps its v2.2 header — wordmark, map, avatar — and the field still carries the area inside it. What changed is the fit: the label can shrink now and sits centred between the magnifier and the area chip, so the pill stays even end to end instead of running off the right edge.
  • Home keeps you oriented. The header fades a night wash in as the hero slides under it, so the wordmark, area, search and avatar never disappear.
  • Depth on the rail. The card nearest the centre sits at full size and its neighbours settle back 5 %, measured in client coordinates so the snapped card is always the flat one. Dots under the rail mark the position; Reduced removes the offsets and keeps the dots as information.
  • One row, one fact each. Thumbnail, kicker and time, title, place and price on one line — and the row still opens with its photo flying into the event hero. That row is the unit the Shortlist pages and the type screens list.
  • Browse by type opens a real screen. Supper club, Music, Fashion and Gallery each push in over Home (Home steps back and dims), with the type's edit copy, a count, a switcher for the other types and its own list. Back slides it away. See all opens the same screen scoped to the tab; Browse by type opens it on the whole catalogue.
  • One origin per screen. Type rows open the same event detail, and Back returns to the list you came from — never to a screen you didn't leave.
  • The event opens from the card itself. The tapped photo flies into the hero; back reverses it.
  • One quiet nav. Over the hero, back and the pills float on glass. Past 140 pt they hand off to a compact bar with the title and the five section tabs.
  • The stub settles, it doesn't pop. It rises 22 pt after the hero lands, which reads as the ticket being printed.
  • Reserving has a beginning and an end. The ring draws while the hold is placed, then a check and the held state. The price strip is the ticket's tear-off, perforated above and below; the hold stamps it Held · 1 h (honey) where the seat count was, with the total for your tickets, and the host's confirmation re-stamps it Confirmed (moss) with one sheen across the strip and View ticket on the button. From further down the page the stub comes back into view first, so the stamp is seen. In the prototype the host confirms 3.6 s later. Reduced: the stamps dissolve in, nothing moves.
  • Live line, reduced. A sliding ticker can't become "slower"; frozen, it shows a cut sentence. So Reduced shows one whole update at a time with when it happened, and ‹ 2/5 › steps back and forward by a dissolve (Apple's Reduce Motion substitute); nothing changes on its own. A tap on the line opens Live now: every update, newest first, the one on Home marked, each with Open to its event. With full motion the same tap opens the same sheet, and Live ❙❙ pauses the ticker (WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide), as does hovering.
  • Fits any screen (v2.4). The stage measures the visible window (including a phone's moving address bar) and scales the whole device, bezel and the control under it, to fit in one screen, up to 150 %. When the panel and the device both fit well they sit side by side; otherwise the device comes first and the panel follows below. The line under the device says the scale and sizes. Fit → Fill this screen makes the screen you are holding the device: no bezel or fake status bar, the app reflows to the real size (split view from 760 pt wide), and ⋮ on the left edge opens these controls. A phone-sized touch screen starts in Fill.
  • Adding tickets after a hold. Held or confirmed is not the end: the stepper stays live upward (never below what you booked, still 6 per person in all). The bar then shows only the extra, "+180.000₫ · 1 more · 5 in all", the button says "Add 1 ticket", and the line says the added tickets wait for the host too. Adding places a new hold, re-stamps the stub Held with the new count, and the host confirms it again.
  • Filters on every type list. Browse by type and See all get their own Filters button beside Soonest: the same sheet as Home, titled "Filter Music", with When, Price and Seats only (the chips already choose the type). It counts matches live, shows a badge, and a short last page says how many sit just outside the filters. Opening a list from Home starts it unfiltered. Rows lost their chevron, and the price sits centred on the row.
  • Swipe signs, as in onboarding. Browse by type and See all lists use the "How banbe works" deck's language: a soft grey tab at the right edge pulses toward the next page (a tap turns it), a springy hop replaces the pulse when the next page is the last, a quieter tab on the left once you can go back, "Swipe to see more ⟶" under the list until your first page turn, and when a list opens the pages lean left for a moment so the next one shows its edge. Reduced: the tabs and the line stay, nothing moves.
  • No blank paper at the end of a list. A short last page (or a one-page list) ends with a card written for where you are, sized to the missing rows so the page never jumps: filters on → "3 more are just outside your filters" + Show all; end of a tab → new tables open on Thursdays + Notify me; a type → the next-biggest type with its count; all types → Host a night; a search → try a host, an area or a dish + Clear search. One row of room gets a single line and one action.
  • Who hosts this list. Under every Browse by type and See all title, the description gives way to a deck of host faces: four overlapping, then +N, with "AEIE Studios, Bàn Dài and 16 more hosts". The deck turns over every few seconds (not with Reduced). A tap opens all the hosts, searchable by name, each with their events; tapping a host opens their profile (photo, role and areas, track record, this week, years on banbe, Follow and Message, a short About, and every event of theirs on this week; ‹ goes back to the host list). "Hosted by" on the event page opens the same profile. The magnifier left of Map turns the title into a search field that filters the list as you type, paged the same way.
  • More than three tickets. 1 · 2 · 3 stay one tap; 4+ becomes a stepper up to 6 per person, you included, or the seats left if fewer. Under it, six dots are the people (you, then guests) with "You + 3 guests · up to 6"; at the cap it says why it stops. 4+ is the fourth slot on every event and every stage; chosen, that slot becomes a small − 4 + stepper in place (in the compact bar 1 · 2 · 3 fold away to make room). On events with 3 seats or fewer it stays visible but quiet, and a tap says how many are left. The Live ticker loops seamlessly: two copies of the line, so the next update is always coming in. Back down to 3 returns to the chips.
  • The ticket bar adds up. Chips set the quantity, and both the total and the CTA multiply the per-ticket price — 2 vé reads 1.700.000₫, not 850.000₫ twice over. Chips past the seats left are disabled.
  • One source per fact. The hero now carries only the photo, the type and the age. Time, place, price and seats live once, in the ticket stub; the bar carries only the running total. The age row no longer contradicts an "All ages" pill.
  • Less furniture. Good to know is a label/value grid, What's included is a numbered editorial list, and the host is one plain row — no cards and no row rules, just one hairline closing each section.
  • Save the date, in either calendar. A slim row inside the ticket adds the event to the banbe schedule (the bell turns into a spring check) or hands it to Apple Calendar — two actions in 50 pt instead of a card of their own.

Why it's easier to use

  • Every target is at least 44 pt; the nav hands off at a scroll position you can feel, not a threshold you have to hunt.
  • Motion only ever explains a change of place — nothing moves that you didn't touch, and nothing delays reading.
  • Reduced motion is a first-class state, not a fallback: Reduced above flattens every duration and removes the scroll depth, and prefers-reduced-motion does the same automatically. Reading progress stays, because it is information.
  • Text contrast moves with the surface: white over photo, ink the moment the bar turns solid.
